## Course 23: AI Video Generation
**Track:** Video & Media | **Pricing:** NGN 35,000 / $49 USD
**Cover Image:** `cover_images/course_23_cover.jpg`

### 1. Course Overview & Target Audience
Generative AI video has entered a cinematic era with diffusion models like Runway Gen-3 Alpha. This course is an end-to-end masterclass on directing, generating, and refining AI video: mastering text-to-video and image-to-video prompt engineering, controlling virtual camera movements with Director Mode, transferring human facial acting via Act-One, performing generative inpainting, applying LUT color grading, and producing cohesive narrative cinematic sequences.

**Who This Course Is For:**
- Filmmakers, directors, and creative directors looking to create cinematic sequences with AI video tools.
- 3D artists, VFX artists, and motion designers expanding into generative diffusion video production.
- Commercial video producers wanting to generate photorealistic B-roll and sci-fi/fantasy environments.
- Learners wanting to master camera control, temporal consistency, and character performance in AI video.

**What You Will Produce:**
- A 60-second cohesive cinematic film trailer with multi-shot narrative continuity.
- A Director Camera Movement Study demonstrating 6 distinct virtual camera controls (pan, tilt, zoom, tracking, arc).
- An Act-One Character Performance piece transferring nuanced human emotion onto an AI character.
- A Post-Production Master Package with color grading (LUTs), generative inpainting, and sound design.

### 5. Structured Lesson Breakdown (8 Lessons)
#### Lesson 1: Runway Gen-3 Architecture, Pricing Models & Project Setup
- **Timestamp:** `00:00` - `06:21` (0s to 381s)
- **Summary & Notes:** Introduces Runway's generative video infrastructure, generation credit management, user interface, and project organization. Compares Gen-3 Alpha vs Turbo models and establishes best practices for organizing narrative shot lists.
- **Practical Activity:** Set up a new Runway project workspace and construct a structured 6-shot cinematic shot list table.
- **Knowledge Check / Reflection:** Why is pre-planning a structured shot list essential when generating AI video with metered computational credits?

#### Lesson 2: Generative Prompting, Text-to-Video & Image-to-Video Foundations
- **Timestamp:** `06:21` - `11:21` (381s to 681s)
- **Summary & Notes:** Masterclass on writing cinematic video prompts. Teaches the 5-component prompt structure: Subject, Environment, Lighting/Atmosphere, Camera Movement, and Style/Render quality. Compares pure Text-to-Video against Image-to-Video workflows.
- **Practical Activity:** Generate 3 video shots using Text-to-Video and 3 shots using Image-to-Video starting from a Midjourney keyframe, comparing consistency.
- **Knowledge Check / Reflection:** Why does Image-to-Video provide vastly superior control over character design and composition compared to text-only prompting?

#### Lesson 3: Cinematic Camera Movements, Angles & Director Controls
- **Timestamp:** `11:21` - `19:07` (681s to 1147s)
- **Summary & Notes:** Deep dive into virtual cinematography. Covers Director Mode parameters: X/Y/Z translation, Pan, Tilt, Zoom, and Roll. Teaches how to simulate complex Hollywood camera movements: tracking dolly shots, sweeping aerial drone arcs, and Dutch angles.
- **Practical Activity:** Create 4 variations of the same scene using 4 distinct camera moves: (1) Slow Dolly In, (2) Fast Orbit Arc, (3) Crane Tilt Down, (4) Tracking Pan.
- **Knowledge Check / Reflection:** How does intentional camera motion elevate AI footage from static motion graphics into compelling cinema?

#### Lesson 4: Act-One Expressive Character Animation & Motion Capture
- **Timestamp:** `19:07` - `23:00` (1147s to 1380s)
- **Summary & Notes:** Comprehensive walkthrough of Runway Act-One. Demonstrates recording a human facial performance on webcam/phone and mapping micro-expressions, blinks, emotional shifts, and dialogue onto stylized 2D/3D AI characters with zero motion capture suits.
- **Practical Activity:** Record a 10-second dramatic monologue and transfer the facial performance onto an alien or historical character model using Act-One.
- **Knowledge Check / Reflection:** How does Act-One solve the notorious 'uncanny valley' dead-eye problem of traditional AI character generation?

#### Lesson 5: Prompting Templates, Scene Expansion & Temporal Consistency
- **Timestamp:** `23:00` - `33:09` (1380s to 1989s)
- **Summary & Notes:** Teaches how to extend 5-second video clips into 10-20 second continuous scenes using Extend Video. Covers maintaining temporal consistency of characters and lighting, and using prompt templates to transition between scene angles seamlessly.
- **Practical Activity:** Take a 5-second generation and use Runway's Extend tool twice to produce a continuous 15-second coherent narrative shot.
- **Knowledge Check / Reflection:** What are the common distortion artifacts that occur during video extension, and how do you mitigate them with prompt constraints?

#### Lesson 6: Inpainting, Object Replacement & Background Removal
- **Timestamp:** `33:09` - `40:50` (1989s to 2450s)
- **Summary & Notes:** Covers generative video inpainting and object cleanup. Demonstrates brush-selecting an unwanted object in a moving video and prompting the AI to replace or seamlessly remove it while maintaining moving shadows and reflections.
- **Practical Activity:** Use video inpainting to remove a modern vehicle from a historical street generation and replace it with a horse carriage.
- **Knowledge Check / Reflection:** Why is video inpainting vastly more computationally complex than static photo inpainting?

#### Lesson 7: Color Grading (LUTs), Motion Tracking & Depth of Field
- **Timestamp:** `40:50` - `46:19` (2450s to 2779s)
- **Summary & Notes:** Explores post-processing within Runway and external NLEs. Covers applying cinematic Lookup Tables (LUTs) for filmic color balance, simulating optical shallow depth-of-field, motion tracking text onto 3D video planes, and frame interpolation.
- **Practical Activity:** Apply a film emulation LUT across 5 disparate AI shots to unify their color palette and contrast ratio.
- **Knowledge Check / Reflection:** How does unified color grading bridge the subtle aesthetic differences between separate AI generations?

#### Lesson 8: Audio Integration, 3D Camera Projection & Final Export
- **Timestamp:** `46:19` - `53:27` (2779s to 3207s)
- **Summary & Notes:** Final assembly of the cinematic piece. Covers sound design synthesis (foley, ambient drones, impact hits), dialogue synchronization, 3D camera projection export, and rendering high-bitrate ProRes/H.264 master deliverables.
- **Practical Activity:** Assemble all generated shots into a 60-second trailer with synchronized sound effects and cinematic musical score.

### 6. Written Learning Materials & Quality Assurance
**Actionable Verification Checklist:**
- [ ] Narrative script and 8-shot storyboard completed.
- [ ] High-resolution keyframe images generated in Midjourney/DALL-E.
- [ ] Director Mode camera moves applied (pan, tilt, zoom).
- [ ] Act-One performance capture tested on character monologue.
- [ ] Video extensions executed with character consistency verified.
- [ ] LUT color grade applied across all shots.
- [ ] Sound design and musical score synchronized in final timeline.

**Common Pitfalls & Fixes:**
- **Mistake:** Writing overloaded prompts with contradictory camera motions
  - **Correction:** Asking for 'dolly in, pan left, zoom out, fast orbit' in one prompt. Fix: Choose ONE clear, deliberate camera movement per shot.
- **Mistake:** Relying only on text prompts for complex characters
  - **Correction:** Generating different faces in every shot. Fix: Always use Image-to-Video with the same reference character portrait.
- **Mistake:** Ignoring motion speed parameters
  - **Correction:** Allowing chaotic, unnatural fast motion. Fix: Lower motion intensity sliders to 2-4 for realistic human physics.
- **Mistake:** Neglecting audio and sound effects
  - **Correction:** Exporting silent AI video. Fix: Layer ambient room tone, foley footsteps, and cinematic risers beneath the score.
